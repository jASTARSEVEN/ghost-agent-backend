COMPLIANCE POLICY DOCUMENT
Customer Service & Support Division
Version 1.0 – Internal Use Only

INTRODUCTION
This document outlines the mandatory customer-interaction, data-handling, operational, and professional conduct standards that all Customer Service Representatives (CSRs) must follow when engaging with customers through phone calls, chat, or any voice-based interaction. These policies apply to all employees, contractors, and temporary workers acting on behalf of the company.

The goal of these policies is to ensure safe, trustworthy, compliant, and customer-first interactions that align with company values, data-protection practices, and legal obligations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1 — CUSTOMER IDENTIFICATION & ACCOUNT VERIFICATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 Mandatory Verification Steps
Before accessing, modifying, or discussing any customer account details, the CSR must perform identity verification using at least two approved methods. Acceptable methods include:
- Confirming the customer’s full name as it appears on the account.
- Requesting the registered email or phone number (CSR must never disclose these fields first).
- Asking for the last 3 characters of a stored address or partial postal code.
CSR must not proceed with account-specific support until the verification is fully completed.

1.2 Prohibited Verification Practices
The CSR must not:
- Reveal full account details before the customer has verified themselves.
- Read out loud complete payment information, full addresses, or full phone numbers.
- Accept unverifiable information such as “I remember ordering last week” as proof of identity.

1.3 Exceptions
Inquiries about general product information, shipping policies, or how-to instructions do not require identity verification.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2 — DATA PRIVACY, SECURITY & HANDLING OF PERSONAL INFORMATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Handling of Personal Data
CSRs must handle customer data according to the following guidelines:
- Only access the information required for resolving the issue.
- Never store or record customer information outside approved systems.
- Avoid repeating sensitive data unless absolutely necessary.
- When reading data back to the customer, summarize rather than quote verbatim (e.g., “the order ending in 823”).

2.2 Sensitive Information Restrictions
The CSR must never request or record:
- Full payment card numbers.
- Complete bank account numbers.
- Government-issued identification numbers.
- Customer passwords, one-time security codes, or authentication tokens.

2.3 Communication Channel Compliance
When communicating sensitive account details, the CSR must:
- Confirm that they are speaking with the correct customer.
- Avoid discussing sensitive information over speakerphone if overhearing is likely.
- Politely decline to provide personal information if another person speaks on behalf of the account owner without authorization.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3 — CUSTOMER COMMUNICATION STANDARDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Tone & Language Requirements
The CSR must:
- Maintain professional, clear, and respectful language.
- Avoid sarcasm, slang, and accusatory tones.
- Use empathetic statements when customers express frustration.
- Provide concise answers without unnecessary technical details.

3.2 Clarity & Transparency
The CSR must clearly explain:
- What actions they are taking in the system.
- Expected timelines for refunds, replacements, or escalations.
- Customers’ responsibilities (e.g., returning damaged items).
- Any limitations due to policy, stock availability, or regional restrictions.

3.3 Prohibited Communication Behaviors
The CSR must not:
- Guess or speculate about unavailable information.
- Promise outcomes that cannot be guaranteed.
- Mention internal workflows (e.g., “the system is slow” or “we are understaffed”).
- Criticize other CSRs, the company, or partners.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4 — ORDER, REFUND & REPLACEMENT POLICIES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Refund Eligibility Requirements
Before initiating a refund, the CSR must confirm:
- Order ownership through verification.
- Item delivery status.
- Refund eligibility based on product category (e.g., perishable, digital, high-value electronics).
- Whether prior refunds have been issued for similar items.

4.2 Replacement Process
The CSR may initiate a replacement only if:
- The product qualifies for replacement under warranty.
- The customer reports damage, defect, or missing parts.
- The customer provides necessary verification details (images may be required in some cases).

4.3 Prohibited Refund Practices
The CSR must not:
- Manually override refund restrictions without authorization.
- Provide refunds for third-party seller products unless the policy permits.
- Issue courtesy refunds exceeding the allowed monthly threshold.
- Offer refunds to non-account holders requesting them on behalf of someone else.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5 — DISCLOSURE & COMMUNICATION OF POLICIES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Mandatory Policy Disclosures
The CSR must inform customers of:
- Return deadlines (e.g., 30 days from delivery).
- Restocking fees when applicable.
- Warranty limits for electronics, appliances, and high-value goods.

5.2 Optional Policy Disclosures
CSRs may provide additional clarification when helpful, such as:
- Packaging requirements for return shipments.
- How refund timelines vary by payment method.

5.3 Forbidden Disclosures
CSRs must not:
- Provide internal policy documents.
- Reveal back-end system notes or private investigation flags.
- Disclose risk scores, fraud alerts, or security checks.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 6 — PROFESSIONAL CONDUCT & BEHAVIOR
------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Required Conduct
The CSR must:
- Remain calm even during difficult interactions.
- Provide fact-based responses.
- Offer alternative solutions when the preferred option is unavailable.
- Document all actions taken during the call accurately.

6.2 Escalations
The CSR must escalate calls when:
- The customer requests a supervisor.
- The issue involves fraud, safety incidents, or legal concerns.
- The customer repeatedly disputes policy explanations and requires managerial review.

6.3 Prohibited Conduct
CSRs must not:
- Disconnect the call abruptly.
- Argue with customers.
- Provide personal opinions (e.g., “I think the seller is unreliable”).

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 7 — SHIPPING, DELIVERY & LOGISTICS COMMUNICATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 Standard Shipping Information Rules
CSRs must:
- Confirm order location using the tracking ID.
- Communicate expected delivery windows according to system data.
- Avoid guaranteeing delivery dates unless the system specifies them.

7.2 Handling Delayed Orders
When an order is delayed, CSRs must:
- Acknowledge the inconvenience.
- Provide a clear explanation from system logs.
- Offer next steps such as replacement, refund, or further tracking.

7.3 Prohibited Shipping Claims
CSRs must not:
- State the courier “lost the package” unless officially confirmed.
- Provide tracking information that doesn’t appear in the system.
- Suggest customers contact shipping carriers directly unless the policy mandates it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 8 — FRAUD PREVENTION & SECURITY PRACTICES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

8.1 Suspicious Activity Handling
CSRs must document and escalate when:
- Multiple high-value items are repeatedly refunded or reported missing.
- Customers request account changes without verification.
- System alerts indicate potential fraud indicators.

8.2 Prohibited Behaviors Regarding Fraud
The CSR must never:
- Accuse a customer of fraud directly.
- Reveal internal fraud-detection mechanisms.
- Disable fraud flags without authorization.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 9 — CALL CLOSURE REQUIREMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Before ending the call, CSRs must:
- Confirm that all issues raised by the customer have been addressed.
- Summarize resolutions (refund initiated, replacement shipped, investigation opened).
- Provide any required next steps.
- Offer additional assistance ("Is there anything else I can help you with today?").
- End with a courteous closing statement.
