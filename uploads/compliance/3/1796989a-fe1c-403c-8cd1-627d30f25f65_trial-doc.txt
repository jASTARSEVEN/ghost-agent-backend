COMPLIANCE POLICY DOCUMENT
Customer Service & Support Division
Version 1.0 – Internal Use Only

INTRODUCTION
This document outlines the mandatory customer-interaction, data-handling, operational, and professional conduct standards that all Customer Service Representatives (CSRs) must follow when engaging with customers through phone calls, chat, or any voice-based interaction. These policies apply to all employees, contractors, and temporary workers acting on behalf of the company.

The goal of these policies is to ensure safe, trustworthy, compliant, and customer-first interactions that align with company values, data-protection practices, and legal obligations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1 — CUSTOMER IDENTIFICATION & ACCOUNT VERIFICATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 Mandatory Verification Steps
Before accessing, modifying, or discussing any customer account details, the CSR must perform identity verification using at least two approved methods. Acceptable methods include:
- Confirming the customer’s full name as it appears on the account.
- Requesting the registered email or phone number (CSR must never disclose these fields first).
- Asking for the last 3 characters of a stored address or partial postal code.
CSR must not proceed with account-specific support until the verification is fully completed.

1.2 Prohibited Verification Practices
The CSR must not:
- Reveal full account details before the customer has verified themselves.
- Read out loud complete payment information, full addresses, or full phone numbers.
- Accept unverifiable information such as “I remember ordering last week” as proof of identity.

1.3 Exceptions
Inquiries about general product information, shipping policies, or how-to instructions do not require identity verification.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2 — DATA PRIVACY, SECURITY & HANDLING OF PERSONAL INFORMATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Handling of Personal Data
CSRs must handle customer data according to the following guidelines:
- Only access the information required for resolving the issue.
- Never store or record customer information outside approved systems.
- Avoid repeating sensitive data unless absolutely necessary.
- When reading data back to the customer, summarize rather than quote verbatim (e.g., “the order ending in 823”).

2.2 Sensitive Information Restrictions
The CSR must never request or record:
- Full payment card numbers.
- Complete bank account numbers.
- Government-issued identification numbers.
- Customer passwords, one-time security codes, or authentication tokens.

2.3 Communication Channel Compliance
When communicating sensitive account details, the CSR must:
- Confirm that they are speaking with the correct customer.
- Avoid discussing sensitive information over speakerphone if overhearing is likely.
- Politely decline to provide personal information if another person speaks on behalf of the account owner without authorization.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3 — CUSTOMER COMMUNICATION STANDARDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Tone & Language Requirements
The CSR must:
- Maintain professional, clear, and respectful language.
- Avoid sarcasm, slang, and accusatory tones.
- Use empathetic statements when customers express frustration.
- Provide concise answers without unnecessary technical details.

3.2 Clarity & Transparency
The CSR must clearly explain:
- What actions they are taking in the system.
- Expected timelines for refunds, replacements, or escalations.
- Customers’ responsibilities (e.g., returning damaged items).
- Any limitations due to policy, stock availability, or regional restrictions.

3.3 Prohibited Communication Behaviors
The CSR must not:
- Guess or speculate about unavailable information.
- Promise outcomes that cannot be guaranteed.
- Mention internal workflows (e.g., “the system is slow” or “we are understaffed”).
- Criticize other CSRs, the company, or partners.
